Purpose: test whether weighted arithmetic history adds predictive information beyond position and small-prime divisibility.
This prediction experiment begins above one thousand to supply complete history windows and does not exercise a start at one. In its arithmetic representation, one has zero logarithmic prime-power weight and zero distinct prime factors.
All features use the current prime, fixed small primes, or numbers at or below the current prime. Labels alone use the next prime. Current-prime membership in the ground-truth list is known at prediction time.
Chronological blocks are fixed in the script. Gaps in numerical coverage separate the distant holdout. Hyperparameters are selected solely on validation. No feature, model hyperparameter, or fit was revised after test inspection. An elementary first-survivor control and exploratory paired uncertainty were added after the initial run to check the apparent small history gain against a stronger baseline. This is an exploratory study.
A source sieve creates data; a second pass derives arithmetic prefixes. Neither is claimed to be avoided by the experimental model. Past lambda and prime-factor features could be maintained incrementally, but those update costs are not benchmarked here.
Exact accuracy is the fraction of immediate successors predicted correctly. Top-five recall reports whether one of five proposed gaps matches; it does not certify a successor. Mean absolute error is in integer positions.
History features include thirty-two preceding prime gaps, equal/linear/exponential weighted summaries, and preceding-integer windows of logarithmic prime-power weights and distinct-prime-factor counts. Linear old-heavy weights increase by one for each step back, as proposed in the conversation.
Position-only uses no divisibility filter. Both arithmetic models mask predictions inconsistent with divisibility by three, five, seven, eleven, or thirteen. This gives the main residue-versus-history comparison identical masking.
A finite improvement would be empirical evidence for this representation only. Failure would not prove that no exact recurrence exists. Both outcomes remain distinct from a proof of an exact general successor rule.
The reported forecasts are one step at a time, supplied with the true current prime and true past history. They do not evaluate repeated rollout using erroneous model outputs. Constructing that history at an arbitrary large initial prime remains additional work.
